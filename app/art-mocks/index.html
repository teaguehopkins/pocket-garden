Pocket Garden: three art directions

Same four tiles (Oak, Sunflower, Frog, Pond), the same plot mid-season with a planting preview, and one weather state per style. Small sizes show how each reads at a glance on a phone. Pick one; the full tile set, tokens and motion follow from it.

A · Seed Packet riso print, three inks

Flat shapes printed in green, yellow and blue that overlap where they cross, slightly off-register like a cheap seed packet. Bold, graphic and very readable; every species can be told apart by silhouette and ink mix alone.

Motion
Ink layers wobble apart on tap, tiles stamp down on plant, rain falls as blue dashes.
Dark mode
Inks glow on navy paper (screen blend instead of multiply).
Effort
Lowest. Three-layer flat shapes per species.

B · Clay Diorama soft toy garden

Chunky, rounded pieces with soft shading and contact shadows, set in a wooden raised bed. Warm and tactile; the board feels like a toy you place things into. Richest for juicy feedback, but busier at small sizes.

Motion
Pieces squash and settle, canopies bob, light sweeps across the bed through the day.
Dark mode
Dusk lighting with a cool moon glow.
Effort
Highest. Gradients and shadows per piece, careful palette per season.

C · Sun Print cyanotype specimen sheet

Plants as pale silhouettes on Prussian blue, like the botanical sun prints of the 1840s, with typewritten specimen labels. Calm and distinctive, a natural fit for a slow, thoughtful game. One colour means groups must be told apart by shape, so tags carry more weight.

Motion
New tiles develop in like an exposure, mist drifts across in Overcast weather.
Light mode
Blue print on white paper (the negative).
Effort
Medium. Detailed silhouettes, one colour, a texture filter.
